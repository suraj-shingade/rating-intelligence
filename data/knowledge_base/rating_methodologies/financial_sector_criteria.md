# CRISIL Rating Criteria: Financial Sector Entities

## 1. Scope and Applicability

This criteria document applies to financial sector entities rated by CRISIL, including commercial banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), housing finance companies (HFCs), microfinance institutions (MFIs), insurance companies, asset management companies, and other regulated financial intermediaries. The methodology addresses long-term ratings, bank loan ratings, and instrument-specific ratings for financial sector entities.

## 2. Business Risk Assessment Framework

### 2.1 Franchise and Market Position

- **Market share**: Assessed by total assets, deposits (for banks), AUM (for NBFCs/HFCs), or gross written premium (for insurance). Top-10 position in the relevant segment is considered strong.
- **Deposit franchise (banks)**: CASA (Current Account Savings Account) ratio as a measure of funding stability and cost. CASA ratio above 40% is considered strong for banking entities.
- **Branch and distribution network**: Geographic reach and penetration depth. Relevant for banks and insurance companies where physical presence drives customer acquisition.
- **Customer base diversity**: Concentration in a single borrower segment, geography, or product type is a risk factor. Retail-focused portfolios are rated more favorably than wholesale-concentrated books.
- **Product breadth**: Ability to cross-sell and generate fee-based income alongside core lending or underwriting.

### 2.2 Asset Quality and Underwriting Standards

- **Gross NPA ratio**: The primary asset quality metric for banks and NBFCs. Assessed against peer medians and industry benchmarks.
- **Net NPA ratio**: Indicates adequacy of provisioning. Net NPA below 1% is considered strong for banking entities.
- **Provision coverage ratio (PCR)**: Percentage of gross NPAs covered by provisions. PCR above 70% is considered adequate; above 80% is strong.
- **Credit cost**: Annual credit cost as a percentage of average assets. Stability and trend are more important than the absolute level.
- **Portfolio granularity**: Average ticket size, geographic distribution of the loan book, and sector concentration.
- **Underwriting standards**: Loan approval processes, risk assessment frameworks, credit scoring models, and post-disbursement monitoring rigor.

### 2.3 Regulatory Environment

- **Capital adequacy requirements**: Compliance with RBI/IRDAI capital norms. Buffer above minimum regulatory requirements is assessed.
- **Regulatory track record**: History of regulatory actions, penalties, or supervisory concerns.
- **Systemically important entity status**: D-SIBs (Domestic Systemically Important Banks) receive implicit government support consideration.

## 3. Financial Risk Assessment Framework

### 3.1 Capital Adequacy

| Metric                        | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|-------------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| CRAR (Banks)                  | >16%          | 13-16%       | 11-13%       | <11%                |
| Tier-1 Capital Ratio (Banks)  | >14%          | 11-14%       | 9-11%        | <9%                 |
| Capital Adequacy (NBFCs)      | >22%          | 18-22%       | 15-18%       | <15%                |
| Net Worth Coverage of Net NPAs| >5x           | 3-5x         | 1.5-3x       | <1.5x               |

Capital adequacy is assessed on a current basis and projected basis, considering planned growth, dividend payout, and expected credit costs.

### 3.2 Profitability

| Metric                        | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|-------------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Return on Assets (ROA)        | >1.5%         | 1.0-1.5%     | 0.5-1.0%     | <0.5%               |
| Return on Equity (ROE)        | >15%          | 10-15%       | 6-10%        | <6%                 |
| Net Interest Margin (NIM)     | >3.5%         | 2.5-3.5%     | 2.0-2.5%     | <2.0%               |
| Cost-to-Income Ratio          | <45%          | 45-55%       | 55-65%       | >65%                |
| Pre-Provision Operating Profit/Average Assets | >3.0% | 2.0-3.0% | 1.5-2.0% | <1.5%            |

Profitability assessment considers structural differences: PSU banks typically have lower ROA but benefit from government ownership support; NBFCs operate at higher NIMs but face higher credit costs; microfinance institutions have the highest NIMs but also the highest credit volatility.

### 3.3 Leverage and Funding

| Metric                        | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|-------------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Debt/Equity (NBFCs)           | <4.0x         | 4.0-6.0x     | 6.0-8.0x     | >8.0x               |
| Gross Gearing (Banks)         | <8.0x         | 8.0-12.0x    | 12.0-15.0x   | >15.0x              |

### 3.4 Asset-Liability Management (ALM)

- **Cumulative mismatch**: Assessed across time buckets (1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year). Negative cumulative mismatch exceeding 15% of cumulative outflows in any bucket is a concern.
- **Funding diversification**: Dependence on a single funding source (e.g., commercial paper, single bank line) is a risk. Entities with diversified funding across deposits, bonds, bank borrowings, and securitization are rated more favorably.
- **Liquidity buffer**: Liquid assets as a percentage of total assets. Minimum 10% liquid asset coverage of short-term obligations is expected.

## 5. Sector-Specific Considerations

### 5.1 Public Sector Banks

- **Government ownership support**: PSU banks benefit from implicit and explicit government support, which provides a rating uplift of 2-4 notches above their standalone credit profile.
- **Capital infusion history**: Track record of government capital infusions to maintain regulatory capital compliance.
- **Merger integration**: For recently merged PSU banks, assessment of integration progress, branch rationalization, and technology harmonization.

### 5.2 Non-Banking Financial Companies

- **Regulatory evolution**: RBI's scale-based regulation (Upper Layer, Middle Layer, Base Layer) introduces differentiated regulatory expectations. Upper Layer NBFCs are subject to bank-like regulatory requirements.
- **Funding concentration risk**: NBFCs without a deposit franchise face higher funding risk. Assessment of funding diversification across banks, capital markets, and securitization.
- **Asset-liability maturity matching**: Critical for NBFCs funding long-tenure assets (housing, infrastructure) with shorter-tenure borrowings.
- **Promoter group support**: Assessment of the parent or promoter group's willingness and ability to provide liquidity and capital support in stress scenarios.

### 5.3 Housing Finance Companies

- **Real estate cycle sensitivity**: HFC portfolios are directly exposed to real estate market cycles. Geographic concentration in markets with elevated price-to-income ratios is a risk.
- **Loan-to-value ratios**: Average LTV at origination and current basis. LTV above 80% at portfolio level indicates elevated risk.
- **Construction finance exposure**: Developer lending (construction finance, LAP to developers) carries higher risk than retail home loans. Assessment of developer portfolio as a percentage of total AUM.

### 5.4 Microfinance Institutions

- **Political and regulatory risk**: MFI sector is vulnerable to state-level political interventions (loan waivers, rate caps). Geographic concentration in states with a history of such interventions is a risk.
- **Client indebtedness levels**: Total indebtedness of the target borrower segment. Over-leveraging of borrowers leads to portfolio stress.
- **Field force management**: Quality and stability of the field workforce. High attrition in collection staff is an early warning indicator.

## 6. Qualitative Overlay for Financial Sector

**Positive qualitative factors (up to +2 notches):**
- Government or quasi-sovereign ownership with demonstrated support history
- Systemic importance (D-SIB status or equivalent)
- Strong parentage (subsidiary of a highly rated domestic or global financial group)
- Exceptional risk management culture with demonstrated performance through credit cycles
- Technology leadership providing cost and distribution advantages

**Negative qualitative factors (up to -2 notches):**
- Governance concerns, regulatory penalties, or compliance failures
- Aggressive growth strategy without commensurate risk infrastructure build-out
- Concentrated exposure to a single sector, geography, or borrower group
- Frequent management changes or promoter group instability
- Weak disclosure practices or significant related-party transactions

## 7. Rating Triggers and Surveillance Thresholds

### 7.1 Downgrade Triggers (Banks)

- GNPA ratio increasing above 5% or net NPA above 2.5%
- CRAR declining below 12% or Tier-1 below 9%
- ROA declining below 0.5% on a sustained basis
- Significant spike in credit costs (>200 bps above the 3-year average)
- Material regulatory action or fraud discovery
- Loss of deposit franchise (CASA ratio declining below 25%)

### 7.2 Downgrade Triggers (NBFCs/HFCs)

- GNPA ratio increasing above 4% or net NPA above 2%
- Capital adequacy declining below 15%
- Debt/Equity exceeding 7.0x
- Liquidity coverage declining below 1.0x for the next 3-month bucket
- Inability to refinance maturing debt within 30 days of maturity
- Significant collection efficiency decline (below 95% for 2+ consecutive months)

### 7.3 Upgrade Triggers

- Sustained improvement in asset quality (GNPA declining 200+ bps from current level)
- Capital adequacy improving to the next rating category threshold
- Demonstrated profitability improvement (ROA improving 50+ bps)
- Material franchise strengthening (geographic expansion, product diversification)
- Resolution of legacy asset quality issues with demonstrated portfolio seasoning

## 8. Data Requirements for Financial Sector Ratings

### 8.1 Mandatory Financial Data

- Audited financial statements for the last 3 fiscal years
- Quarterly unaudited results (for listed entities)
- RBI/IRDAI regulatory returns and compliance certificates
- Asset-liability maturity profile
- Segment-wise portfolio composition and asset quality
- Top-20 borrower exposure list (with ratings where available)
- Liquidity position statement (cash, liquid investments, undrawn lines)

### 8.2 Mandatory Business Data

- Branch/distribution network details
- Product-wise AUM/portfolio breakup
- Geographic distribution of portfolio
- Customer acquisition cost and channel mix
- Technology platform details
- Risk management framework and credit approval process documentation
- Board composition and key management profiles
