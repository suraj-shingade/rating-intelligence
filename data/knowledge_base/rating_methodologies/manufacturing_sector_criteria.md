# CRISIL Rating Criteria: Manufacturing Sector

## 1. Scope and Applicability

This criteria document applies to all manufacturing sector entities rated by CRISIL, encompassing steel, cement, automotive, chemicals, textiles, pharmaceuticals, consumer goods, and diversified industrial conglomerates. The methodology addresses both long-term and bank loan rating assessments for manufacturing companies operating primarily in the Indian market.

## 2. Business Risk Assessment Framework

### 2.1 Industry Risk

Industry risk assessment evaluates the structural characteristics of the manufacturing sub-sector, including:

- **Demand cyclicality**: Degree to which demand is linked to GDP cycles, capex cycles, or discretionary spending. Steel and cement exhibit high cyclicality; pharmaceuticals and FMCG exhibit low cyclicality.
- **Competitive intensity**: Degree of fragmentation, import competition, pricing power dynamics, and barriers to entry. Sectors with high fixed costs and commodity characteristics (steel, cement) face intense competition.
- **Regulatory environment**: Impact of government policy on pricing (drug price control, cement pricing scrutiny), environmental compliance costs, import/export duties, and sector-specific regulations.
- **Input cost volatility**: Exposure to raw material price fluctuations (crude oil for chemicals, iron ore for steel, cotton for textiles). Companies with limited pass-through ability face elevated risk.
- **Technology and obsolescence risk**: Pace of technological change and capital intensity of staying competitive.

### 2.2 Competitive Position

The entity's competitive position is assessed on:

- **Market share and scale**: Top-5 position in the domestic market is considered strong. Scale provides procurement advantages, distribution reach, and cost efficiencies.
- **Cost position**: Relative cost competitiveness versus domestic and import peers. Proximity to raw material sources, energy costs, and labor productivity are key differentiators.
- **Brand and product differentiation**: Ability to command premium pricing. Relevant for consumer-facing manufacturing (FMCG, automotive, branded textiles) but less material for commodity manufacturers.
- **Customer concentration**: Revenue dependency on top-5 customers. Concentration above 40% in a single customer is considered elevated risk.
- **Geographic diversification**: Export revenue as a percentage of total revenue. Companies with 25%+ export revenue benefit from demand diversification but face currency risk.

### 2.3 Management and Governance

- **Track record**: Minimum 10 years of operating history preferred for investment-grade consideration. Demonstrated ability to navigate industry downturns.
- **Promoter commitment**: Clarity of promoter vision, capital infusion track record, and related-party transaction governance.
- **Board independence**: Compliance with SEBI LODR requirements. Presence of independent directors with relevant industry expertise.
- **Succession planning**: Documented succession plans for key management positions.

## 3. Financial Risk Assessment Framework

### 3.1 Profitability Metrics

| Metric                   | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|--------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| EBITDA Margin            | >18%          | 12-18%       | 8-12%        | <8%                 |
| ROCE                     | >20%          | 14-20%       | 10-14%       | <10%                |
| PAT Margin               | >10%          | 6-10%        | 3-6%         | <3%                 |

Profitability thresholds are sector-adjusted. Capital-intensive sectors (steel, cement) have structurally lower margins; asset-light manufacturing (branded consumer goods) is held to higher thresholds.

### 3.2 Leverage Metrics

| Metric                   | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|--------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Debt/Equity              | <0.8x         | 0.8-1.5x     | 1.5-2.5x     | >2.5x               |
| Debt/EBITDA              | <2.0x         | 2.0-3.5x     | 3.5-5.0x     | >5.0x               |
| Total Debt/Total Assets  | <0.35         | 0.35-0.50    | 0.50-0.65    | >0.65               |

Leverage assessment considers the nature of debt (long-term project debt vs. short-term working capital), currency of borrowing, and hedging arrangements for foreign currency debt.

### 3.3 Coverage Metrics

| Metric                   | CRISIL AAA/AA | CRISIL A     | CRISIL BBB   | CRISIL BB and below |
|--------------------------|---------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Interest Coverage (EBITDA/Interest) | >6.0x | 3.5-6.0x | 2.0-3.5x | <2.0x               |
| DSCR                     | >2.0x         | 1.5-2.0x     | 1.2-1.5x     | <1.2x               |

Interest coverage is the primary coverage metric. DSCR is relevant for project finance and term loan assessments where principal repayment schedules are defined.

### 3.4 Liquidity Assessment

| Metric                   | Strong         | Adequate     | Stretched    | Weak                |
|--------------------------|----------------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Current Ratio            | >1.5x          | 1.2-1.5x     | 1.0-1.2x     | <1.0x               |
| Cash/Short-term Debt     | >0.5x          | 0.3-0.5x     | 0.1-0.3x     | <0.1x               |
| Working Capital Cycle    | Improving       | Stable       | Elongating   | Severely stretched  |

Liquidity assessment also considers undrawn bank lines, liquid investments, and the seasonality of cash flows relative to debt repayment schedules.

## 4. Rating Scale Mapping for Manufacturing Sector

### 4.1 Long-Term Rating Scale

| Rating Category  | Financial Profile                                                  | Business Profile                                                    |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| CRISIL AAA       | Debt/Equity <0.5x, Interest Coverage >8x, EBITDA Margin >20%     | Market leader, strong brand, high diversification, >20 years track record |
| CRISIL AA        | Debt/Equity <0.8x, Interest Coverage >6x, EBITDA Margin >15%     | Top-3 market position, established brand, moderate diversification  |
| CRISIL A         | Debt/Equity <1.5x, Interest Coverage >3.5x, EBITDA Margin >12%   | Established player, recognized brand, adequate diversification      |
| CRISIL BBB       | Debt/Equity <2.5x, Interest Coverage >2.0x, EBITDA Margin >8%    | Meaningful market presence, limited diversification                 |
| CRISIL BB        | Debt/Equity 2.5-4.0x, Interest Coverage 1.5-2.0x                 | Small-scale operations, high customer/product concentration         |
| CRISIL B         | Debt/Equity >4.0x, Interest Coverage <1.5x                       | Subscale, limited competitive advantages, thin margin business      |

### 4.2 Bank Loan Rating Considerations

Bank loan ratings incorporate additional factors:
- **Collateral coverage**: Asset coverage ratio for secured facilities
- **Working capital management**: Utilization patterns of fund-based limits
- **Banking relationship**: Track record with the lending bank, operational account conduct
- **Facility structure**: Term loan amortization schedule alignment with cash flow generation

## 5. Sector-Specific Adjustments

### 5.1 Steel Manufacturing

- Cyclicality adjustment: Financial thresholds are evaluated through-the-cycle rather than at peak or trough
- Capacity utilization: Minimum 70% utilization expected for investment-grade ratings
- Integration level: Captive raw material sources (iron ore, coal) provide significant rating uplift
- Export orientation: Companies with 30%+ export revenue benefit from demand diversification but face trade policy risks (anti-dumping duties)

### 5.2 Cement Manufacturing

- Regional market dynamics: India's cement market is regional; transportation costs limit effective competition radius to 300-400 km
- Capacity additions: Large capacity additions relative to regional demand are viewed negatively
- Fuel cost management: Access to captive power, waste heat recovery, and alternative fuels is a differentiator
- Land and limestone reserves: Adequacy of limestone reserves for 20+ years of operations at current capacity

### 5.3 Automotive Manufacturing

- OEM vs. component supplier distinction: OEMs rated on product portfolio breadth and brand equity; component suppliers on customer concentration and technology content
- Model cycle risk: Dependency on a single model platform elevates risk
- Electrification transition: Assessment of electric vehicle strategy and capital allocation toward EV capabilities
- Channel and dealer network: Breadth and health of the distribution network

### 5.4 Chemicals Manufacturing

- Feedstock integration: Backward integration into key feedstocks provides margin stability
- Product portfolio depth: Specialty chemicals rated more favorably than commodity chemicals due to higher margins and lower cyclicality
- Environmental compliance: Chemical manufacturing faces elevated environmental compliance costs; history of environmental incidents is a negative factor
- Regulatory dependency: Companies dependent on agrochemical approvals face binary regulatory risk

## 6. Qualitative Overlay and Notching

The quantitative financial assessment provides the base rating indication. Qualitative factors can adjust the indicated rating by up to two notches in either direction:

**Positive qualitative factors (up to +2 notches):**
- Market leadership with demonstrated pricing power
- Strong promoter group with established track record of capital support
- Exceptional geographic or product diversification
- Demonstrated ability to maintain margins through industry downturns

**Negative qualitative factors (up to -2 notches):**
- Governance concerns or related-party transaction opacity
- Concentrated customer or product dependency
- Regulatory or environmental compliance issues
- Weak financial disclosures or accounting quality concerns
- Aggressive acquisition strategy with integration risk

## 7. Rating Triggers and Surveillance Thresholds

### 7.1 Downgrade Triggers

- Debt/EBITDA increasing above 4.0x on a sustained basis
- Interest coverage declining below 2.0x
- EBITDA margin contraction exceeding 500 basis points from the rating assumption
- Debt/Equity increasing above 2.0x for investment-grade rated entities
- Material adverse regulatory action or environmental compliance failure
- Loss of a customer contributing more than 20% of revenue without replacement
- Significant unplanned capex increasing leverage beyond rating assumptions

### 7.2 Upgrade Triggers

- Debt/EBITDA declining below 2.0x on a sustained basis
- Interest coverage exceeding 5.0x consistently
- Meaningful improvement in market position or business diversification
- Demonstrated ability to maintain profitability through a full industry cycle
- Deleveraging from organic cash flow (not equity infusion) over 2+ consecutive years

## 8. Data Requirements for Manufacturing Sector Ratings

### 8.1 Mandatory Financial Data

- Audited financial statements for the last 3 fiscal years
- Provisional/management financials for the current year
- Financial projections for the next 2 fiscal years
- Debt maturity profile
- Capex plan and funding sources
- Working capital analysis (debtor days, creditor days, inventory days)

### 8.2 Mandatory Business Data

- Product-wise revenue breakup
- Customer concentration (top-5 and top-10 customers as percentage of revenue)
- Installed capacity and utilization rates
- Raw material procurement strategy and key supplier dependencies
- Export/domestic revenue split
- Competitive positioning data (market share, ranking)
- Management profiles and organizational structure
